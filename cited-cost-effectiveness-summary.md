## Detailed Findings with Citations

### One Acre Fund

**Program:** Bundled agricultural services (seeds, fertilizer, training, insurance, market facilitation)

**Cost Data:**
- Full program cost: **$80 per farmer** [S5]
- Farmer loan repayment (2024): **$43 average** [S2]
- Net donation needed (2024): **$19.59 per farmer** [S2]
  - Based on: 96% repayment rate covering 74% of program costs
  - Accounts for field revenues

**Income Impact Data:**
- 2022-2024 average: **+$124 per year** (+35% income increase) [S1]
  - Source: "One Acre Fund farmers saw an average increase of ~35% in farm income, or about US$124 in incremental profit each year" - Global Innovation Fund

- 2021 data: **+$104 additional profit** (+45% increase) [S3]
  - Source: "In 2021, One Acre Fund helped 1.44 million farmers earn an average of $104 in additional profit (which represents a profit increase of 45%)" - Focusing Philanthropy

**Yield vs Income:**
- Harvest yields: **2× (100% increase)** on average [S5]
  - Source: "Farmers on the One Acre Fund program double their harvest yields on average compared to their prior output"

- But farm income: Only **40-45% increase** after repayment [S5]
  - Source: "Participants see roughly a 40%+ increase in farm income on supported land, even after repayment of program costs"

- **Why the gap?** Yield doubling doesn't equal income doubling due to:
  - Input costs (seeds, fertilizer)
  - Loan repayment to One Acre Fund
  - Labor costs
  - Not all land treated
  - Market price effects

**Calculated Cost to Double Income:**
- Using $80 cost & 35% gain: **$229** to double income
  - Calculation: $80 × (100% ÷ 35%) = $228.57 ≈ $229

- Using $19.59 net cost & 35% gain: **$56** to double income
  - Calculation: $19.59 × (100% ÷ 35%) = $55.97 ≈ $56
  - Caveat: $19.59 may not represent full marginal cost

- Using $80 cost & 45% gain (2021): **$178** to double income
  - Calculation: $80 × (100% ÷ 45%) = $177.78 ≈ $178

**Tree Program:**
- Cost: **$20-30 per family** (one-time) [S4]
  - Source: "$20 per family — invested once — can equip a farmer with many dozens of shrubs and trees"

- Income effects: **Unknown** (under study) [S6], [S7]
  - GiveWell funded $1.3M RCT in Rwanda to measure long-term income impacts
  - Evidence shows trees planted increase after one year
  - No direct causal evidence of long-term income/profit effects yet

**Scale & Funding Capacity:**
- Current reach: **1.4+ million farmers** [S3]
- Can absorb: **$5m+ easily** (large operational scale)

**Fungibility:**
- Major funders: Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, Global Innovation Fund [multiple sources]
- Historical: GiveWell Standout Charity (2020-2021, designation discontinued)
- Risk level: **MODERATE-HIGH**

**Funding Gap Context:**
- Global estimate: **$151 billion annual gap** for smallholder farmer support [S8]
- Based on: $300 per farmer annual investment needed
- Current available: **~$2 billion** globally
- Source: One Acre Fund report, November 2024

**BOTEC Verdict:** ❌ **FAIL**
- All variants exceed $30 threshold
- Best case: $56 (still 1.9× over budget)
- Typical case: $178-229 (5.9-7.6× over budget)

### myAgro

**Program:** Mobile layaway savings platform for seeds, fertilizer, tools, and training via SMS

**Cost Data:**
- Cost per farmer: **UNKNOWN - CRITICAL DATA GAP**
- Funding received: $225k initial grant [S9], $2.2M from Mulago Foundation [S11]
- Cannot calculate cost-per-farmer from these figures

**Income Impact Data:**
- 2020 average: **+$178 net income** (+26% increase) [S9]
  - Source: "myAgro's 2020 impact analysis demonstrated that on average, myAgro farmers grew 78% more and earned $178 more net income than control farmers, representing a 26% increase in income"

- Reported range: **$150-300 per farmer** net farming income increase [S9]
  - Source: "Net farming income increases $150-$300 per farmer through this bank-less savings scheme"

- Female saving groups: **$73 net profit** (>100% income increase over control) [S10]
  - Source: "myAgro female saving group members grew 115% more food than control farmers, and earned $73 in net profit, which is more than a 100% increase over control farm income"

**Yield Data:**
- 2020 average: **78% yield increase** [S9]
- Overall program: **50-100% yield increase** (range) [S9]
  - Source: "Results show an astounding increase in yields as well as in family income of between 50% and 100%"

**Calculated Baseline Income:**
- From 26% increase = $178 gain:
  - Implied baseline: $178 ÷ 0.26 = **$685**

- From female groups >100% increase = $73 gain:
  - Implied baseline: **<$73** (since more than doubling)

**Calculated Cost to Double Income:**
- **CANNOT CALCULATE** without knowing cost per farmer
- Scenarios:
  - If cost is $30: Would need to verify 100%+ income gain is achievable at scale
  - If cost is $50: Cost to double at 26% gain = $50 × (100÷26) = $192 (FAIL)
  - If cost is $80: Cost to double at 26% gain = $80 × (100÷26) = $308 (FAIL)

**Scale & Funding Capacity:**
- Current reach: **Hundreds of thousands** of farmers [S9]
- 2025 goal: **1 million farmers annually** [S11]
- Geographic reach: Senegal, Mali, expanding to 2 new West African countries [S11]
- Partnership reach: **4+ million farmers** via CRS, Stromme Foundation, Aga Khan Foundation [S12]

**Fungibility:**
- Major funders: Mulago Foundation, partner NGOs [S11], [S12]
- NOT primarily Gates-funded (unlike One Acre Fund, Precision Dev)
- Risk level: **LOW**

**BOTEC Verdict:** ⚠️ **UNKNOWN - NEEDS VALIDATION**
- Female saving groups show >100% income increase
- If cost ≤$30 per farmer, would **PASS BOTEC**
- If cost $50-80, would **FAIL BOTEC** but still competitive

**PRIORITY ACTION:** **Request detailed cost-per-farmer breakdown from myAgro**

### Precision Development (PxD)

**Program:** Mobile phone-based agricultural advisory via SMS, providing farmers with high-impact digital information

**Cost Data:**
- Annual cost: **$1.80 per household per year** [S13]
  - Source: "A preliminary report by GiveWell concluded that it costs PxD around $1.80 per year to reach a household with its messaging"

- 10-year cost: **$18.00** per household
- 20-year cost: **$36.00** per household

**Income Impact Data:**
- India flagship service: **$12-19 agricultural profit per $1 invested** (long-run) [S14]
  - Source: "A recent study of their flagship service in India estimates that in the long run, every $1 invested in the service will generate agricultural profits of $12 – $19"
  - **Interpretation unclear:** This may be BCR (benefit-cost ratio), not annual return
  - If BCR 12-19:1, and cost is $1.80/year, total benefits = $21.60-34.20

- GiveWell comparison: **~6× as cost-effective as GiveDirectly** [S13]
  - GiveDirectly baseline: ~$1,000 per household
  - Implication: Equivalent benefit for ~$167, but exact mechanism unclear

**Africa-specific data:**
- **NOT AVAILABLE in public sources**
- India data may not generalize to Africa
- Recent MoU with Ethiopia [S17] but no cost-effectiveness data yet

**Calculated Cost to Double Income:**
- **UNCERTAIN** due to measurement methodology questions
- If annual cost $1.80 and benefits are sustained:
  - 10-year scenario: $18 total cost
  - If this achieves income doubling: **PASS BOTEC**
- But: Effects may require ongoing engagement (not one-time)

**Scale & Funding Capacity:**
- Can reach: **Millions via mobile technology** (highly scalable)
- Current operations: Multiple countries

**Fungibility:**
- Major funder: **Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation** ($3M for Ethiopia, Dec 2025-Feb 2027) [S17]
- Recent MoU: Ethiopian Ministry of Agriculture (June 2025) [S17]
- Historical GiveWell engagement:
  - 2020-2021: **Standout Charity** [S13]
  - March 2022: **$539,864 scoping grant** [S15]
  - March 2023: **$700,000 core operations grant** [S16]
  - **BUT: GiveWell declined to fund implementation after scoping** [S16]
- Risk level: **HIGH**

**Critical Red Flag:** 🚩
- GiveWell decided "not to recommend funding for implementation and impact evaluations that PxD proposed after its scoping work" [S16]
- **This requires investigation** - Why did GiveWell walk away?

**BOTEC Verdict:** ✅ **POSSIBLY PASS** (with major caveats)
- Low annual cost ($1.80) suggests potential
- But: GiveWell's 2023 decline is concerning
- And: Measurement methodology unclear
- And: High fungibility with Gates funding

**PRIORITY ACTION:** **Investigate why GiveWell declined 2023 implementation funding**

### Government Fertilizer Subsidy Programs

**Program:** Government-run subsidized fertilizer distribution

**Cost Data:**
- Total spending: **$1 billion annually** across 10 African governments [S18]
- Per program: **$100-160 million/year** [S18]
- Beneficiaries: **Millions** per country [S18]

**Income Impact Data:**

**Malawi:**
- Effect: **10 kg subsidy → $1.40-1.86 per year** income increase [S18]
- Source: "A 10 kg increase in subsidized fertilizer acquired per household results in a US $1.40 per year increase in average household income, and a US $1.86 per year increase for households who sold their labor before the program was scaled up"

**Senegal:**
- Effect: **11% increase in total gross margin** [S19]
- Source: "Access to subsidized fertilizer is associated with an increase in farmers' total gross margin of 11%"

**Ghana:**
- Effect: **$332 increase in gross maize income** [S20]
- Source: "The adoption of only fertilizer increases gross maize income by $332 and improves per capita consumption significantly by 1.22"

**Cost-Effectiveness Assessment:**
- Research consensus: **"Costs generally outweigh benefits"** [S18]
- Source: "The weight of evidence indicates that the costs of programs generally outweigh their benefits, and the overall production and welfare effects tend to be smaller than expected"

**Calculated Cost to Double Income (Malawi):**
- Estimated: **$1,400-2,900** per farmer
- Calculation basis:
  - If 10kg subsidy costs government $10-20 (market-based estimate)
  - And generates $1.40 income gain
  - To double $200 baseline income would need ~$200 gain
  - Required: ($200 ÷ $1.40) × $10-20 = $1,429-2,857

**BOTEC Verdict:** ❌ **FAIL DRAMATICALLY**
- 47-97× over budget
- Research consensus confirms poor cost-effectiveness

**Recommendation:** **AVOID** - Not suitable for effective altruism-oriented funding

### CGIAR Agricultural R&D

**Program:** International agricultural research consortium

**Cost-Benefit Data:**

**Sub-Saharan Africa:**
- BCR: **$1 generates $6 benefit** [S21]
  - Source: "Every dollar spent on international agricultural research in Africa adds about 6 dollars to SSA agricultural output in present value terms"

- Internal Rate of Return: **58% per year** [S22]
  - Source: "CGIAR spending generated an internal rate of return of 58 percent per year—or roughly $6 of benefits for every $1 spent in Sub-Saharan Africa"

**Overall:**
- BCR: **10:1** (cited in multiple sources) [S22]

**Impact Scale:**
- 2016-2020: **221 million hectares** adoption across Asia, Africa, Latin America [S23]
- Economic welfare gains: **$47 billion annually** [S23]
- Source: "By 2016–2020, CGIAR-related crop technologies had been adopted on at least 221 million hectares across Asia, Africa and Latin America, generating economic welfare gains of $47 billion annually"

**Cost per QALY:**
- **$304 per QALY** [S24]
- Comparison: GiveWell health interventions = $45-115 per DALY
- CGIAR is 2.6-6.8× LESS cost-effective than top health interventions

**Why This Doesn't Fit the BOTEC:**
- R&D benefits are **diffuse** across millions of farmers
- Benefits accrue over **decades**, not immediately
- **Indirect** effects through technology adoption
- Cannot attribute "$30 to double one person's income"

**Example:**
- $1M in drought-resistant maize R&D
- Over 20 years: Generates $6M in increased output
- Spread across 100,000 farmers = $60 per farmer over 20 years = $3/year
- If baseline income $500, this is 0.6% increase per farmer
- Valuable but not "income doubling" for individuals

**BOTEC Verdict:** N/A - **INAPPROPRIATE MODEL**
- R&D investment ≠ Direct implementation
- Wrong framework for "double income for $30" criterion

**Recommendation:** **NOT SUITABLE** for this specific funding opportunity

## EA/GiveWell Benchmarks with Citations

### Malaria Nets (AMF)
- **Cost:** $45-115 per DALY averted [S34]
- **Source:** GiveWell top charity methodology
- **Most cost-effective health intervention**

### GiveWell Bednets (Utility Equivalent)
- **Cost:** ~$500 to produce utility equivalent of doubling income for 1 year [S33]
- **Source:** "For every ~$500 spent on bednets, you produce financial utility equivalent to increasing an individual's ln(consumption) by one unit for one year—which means ~doubling their income for a year" - EA Forum analysis

### GiveDirectly (Cash Transfers)
- **Cost:** ~$1,000 per household [S34]
- **Source:** GiveWell baseline comparison
- **Used as benchmark for cost-effectiveness comparisons**

### Open Philanthropy Bar
- **Standard:** ~1,000× GiveDirectly cost-effectiveness for new programs (as of 2019) [S32]
- **DALY value:** $100,000 [S32]
- **Source:** Open Phil framework updates

### Agriculture vs. Health
- **Health interventions:** $45-115 per DALY (best)
- **Agriculture R&D:** $304 per QALY (much worse) [S24]
- **Conclusion:** Agriculture is less cost-effective than top health interventions on DALY basis

### Agriculture vs. Cash Transfers
- **GiveDirectly:** $1,000 for cash transfer
- **One Acre Fund:** $178 for income doubling (equivalent to 2× $89 per doubling)
  - **2.8× more cost-effective than GiveDirectly** on income basis
- **myAgro:** If $50 cost achieves doubling, **10× more cost-effective**
- **Precision Dev:** If $18 over 10 years achieves doubling, **28× more cost-effective**

**Conclusion:** Agriculture interventions can be **more cost-effective than cash transfers** but generally **less effective than top health interventions**, and **don't meet the $30 BOTEC criterion**.
