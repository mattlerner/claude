## Key Criteria

This analysis evaluates opportunities against three requirements:

1. ✅ **Funding Capacity:** Can counterfactually absorb $5m+ without funging other donors
2. ✅ **Low Fungibility:** Not crowding out existing major funders
3. ❌ **Cost-Effectiveness:** $30 or less to double someone's income (BOTEC)

**Finding:** The $30 BOTEC criterion appears extremely difficult to meet in agricultural productivity interventions.

---

## Top Findings

### Verdict: No Perfect Matches

**No opportunities meet all three criteria.** The binding constraint is the $30 BOTEC.

### Best Candidates (if BOTEC relaxed):

1. **myAgro** - Highest potential, needs cost validation
   - May achieve income doubling for $30-80
   - Female saving groups show >100% income gains
   - Can absorb $5m+, low fungibility
   - **Critical data gap:** Exact cost per farmer

2. **One Acre Fund** - Most proven, costs too high
   - $80 per farmer for 40-45% income gain
   - Would need ~$160-180 for income doubling
   - Can absorb $5m+ easily
   - Moderate-high fungibility (Gates-funded)

3. **Precision Development** - Low cost, unclear impact
   - $1.80/year per household
   - India data promising ($12-19 return per $1)
   - GiveWell declined implementation funding in 2023 🚩
   - High fungibility (Gates-funded Ethiopia program)

## Strategic Recommendations

### Recommended: Investigate Then Deploy

**Timeline:** 2-6 months
**Budget:** $5M + $200-500k research costs

**Approach:**
1. Commission detailed cost-effectiveness analysis of myAgro
2. Request program-specific data from One Acre Fund
3. Investigate why GiveWell declined Precision Development funding
4. Deploy $2-3M to top candidate if validation succeeds
5. Reserve $1-2M for evaluation and future opportunities

**Rationale:** $30 BOTEC may be achievable by myAgro's female saving group model, but needs validation. Conservative, evidence-based approach.

### Alternative: Relax BOTEC to $80-150

**Rationale:**
- $30 may be structurally impossible given input costs
- $80-150 for 50-100% income gains is still highly cost-effective
- Comparable to GiveDirectly, competitive with other development interventions

**Unlocks:**
- One Acre Fund deployment immediately viable
- myAgro likely viable
- Multiple other implementers accessible
- Can deploy $5M without delay

## Context: Why This Is Difficult

### The $30 Challenge

**Agricultural input costs are structural:**
- Seeds: $10-20
- Fertilizer: $20-40
- Training: $10-20
- Logistics: $10-20
- **Total: $60-100+ per farmer**

**Even with cost reducers:**
- Farmer loan repayment: Reduces net cost to $20-40
- Digital delivery: Can get to $2-5
- Still difficult to achieve full income doubling at $30

### Comparison to Other Interventions

| Intervention | Cost | Outcome | Cost-Effectiveness |
|-------------|------|---------|-------------------|
| GiveWell bednets | $45-115 | 1 DALY averted | Best |
| GiveDirectly | $1,000 | Cash transfer | Baseline |
| Bednets (utility) | $500 | 1-year income doubling equiv. | Good |
| One Acre Fund | $80 | 40-45% income gain (multi-year) | Competitive |
| myAgro (if validated) | $30-80? | 50-100% income gain | Potentially very good |
| Fertilizer subsidies | $400+ | 11-30% income gain | Poor |

**Conclusion:** Agriculture is less cost-effective than top health interventions but competitive with cash transfers, especially if multi-year income gains.

## Methodology

- Income effects vary by geography, crop, farmer characteristics
- Multi-year sustainability data limited for some programs
- Fungibility assessments are estimates based on public funding information
