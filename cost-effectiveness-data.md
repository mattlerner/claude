## Key Organizations - Cost & Impact Data

### One Acre Fund

**Costs:**
- Full program cost per farmer: $80
- Farmer loan repayment (average): $43
- Net donation required per farmer: $37 (2024)
- After field revenues: $19.59 (2024 reported)
- Tree program (one-time): $20-30

**Income Impacts:**
- Average farm income increase: $124/year
- Percentage increase: +35% (2022-2024 average)
- 2021 results: $104 additional profit (+45%)
- Yield increase: 2x (100%) on supported land
- **Net income increase: 40-45%** (after repayment)

**Scale:**
- Current farmers served: 1.4+ million
- Geographic reach: Multiple East/West African countries
- Expansion capacity: High

**Key Funders:**
- Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation
- Global Innovation Fund
- Historically: GiveWell (not current top charity)
- Multiple other foundations

**Extrapolated BOTEC:**
- Cost to achieve 100% income doubling: **~$160-180**
- Based on: $80 cost / 45% increase = $177 per 100% increase

### Precision Development (PxD)

**Costs:**
- Cost per household per year: $1.80 (GiveWell 2020 estimate)
- 10-year cost per household: $18
- One-time setup + multi-year delivery model

**Income Impacts:**
- India flagship service: $12-19 agricultural profit per $1 invested (long-run)
- Multiple RCTs show positive production, profit, climate resilience effects
- **Africa-specific income data: UNKNOWN**

**Scale:**
- Can reach millions via mobile technology
- Currently operating in multiple countries
- Recent MoU: Ethiopia digital agriculture transformation

**Key Funders:**
- Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation ($3M for Ethiopia, Dec 2025-Feb 2027)
- Historically: GiveWell grants (2022: $539k, 2023: $700k)
- **Note: GiveWell declined implementation funding in 2023**

**BOTEC:**
- At $1.80/year, if generates $20-30/year in profit: highly cost-effective
- Over 10 years: $18 cost could yield significant cumulative income gains
- **STATUS: Promising but unclear why GiveWell declined funding**

## Comparison Benchmarks

### GiveWell Standards

**Top Charity Benchmark:**
- Malaria nets (AMF): $45-115 per DALY averted
- Utility benchmark: ~$500 to produce equivalent of doubling income for 1 year

**Historical Agriculture Assessment:**
- Precision Development (2020-2021): "~6x as cost-effective as GiveDirectly"
- GiveDirectly baseline: ~$1,000 per household cash transfer
- Implied Precision Dev value: ~$167 per household for equivalent benefit

**CGIAR Agricultural R&D:**
- $304 per QALY
- Much worse than health interventions ($45-115/DALY)
- Not recommended for EA-focused giving

### EA Community Benchmarks

**Open Philanthropy:**
- Values DALY at $100,000 (2 natural log units of income)
- Current bar for new programs: ~1,000x cash transfers
- Agriculture generally doesn't meet this bar

**Copenhagen Consensus:**
- Ranked agricultural R&D 6th among social investments
- Behind: Deworming, micronutrients
- Ahead: Conditional cash transfers for education

## Government/Large-Scale Programs

### Fertilizer Subsidy Programs (NOT RECOMMENDED)

**Costs:**
- 10 African governments: $1 billion annually combined
- Individual programs: $100-160 million/year
- Millions of beneficiaries

**Income Impacts:**
- **Malawi:** 10kg subsidy → $1.40-1.86/year income increase (terrible)
- **Senegal:** 11% increase in gross margin
- **Ghana:** $332 increase in gross maize income

**Assessment:**
- Research consensus: Costs outweigh benefits
- Poor cost-effectiveness
- **Avoid for EA-focused funding**

### CGIAR Research

**Cost-Benefit:**
- Overall BCR: 10:1
- Sub-Saharan Africa: $6 benefit per $1 spent
- Annual IRR: 58% (SSA)

**Impact Scale:**
- 2016-2020: 221M hectares adoption
- $47 billion annual welfare gains

**Assessment:**
- Good ROI for research funding
- But: Diffuse benefits, long timeframes, high fungibility
- **Not suitable for "double income for $30" BOTEC**

## Income Doubling Evidence

### Programs Showing >80% Income Gains:

1. **myAgro female saving groups:** >100% income increase
   - Cost: Unknown
   - Sample: Subset of myAgro participants

2. **One Acre Fund harvest yields:** 2x (100%) yield increase
   - Income increase: Only 40-45% (not equivalent to yield)
   - Cost: $80 per farmer

3. **Farm to Market Alliance (mentioned):** Potential 2x yields
   - Loans: $45 for hybrid seed, fertilizer, herbicide
   - Income data: Not available

### Why Yield ≠ Income:

- Yields may double, but income increases less due to:
  - Input costs (must repay loans for seed/fertilizer)
  - Labor costs
  - Market price effects
  - Not all land is treated

## Cost Structure Breakdown

### Why Agricultural Interventions Cost $50-100+

**Typical Bundle Components:**

| Component | Cost Range |
|-----------|------------|
| Quality seeds | $10-20 |
| Fertilizer | $20-40 |
| Training & extension | $10-20 |
| Logistics & delivery | $10-20 |
| Insurance (some programs) | $5-10 |
| Monitoring & evaluation | $5-10 |
| **Total** | **$60-120** |

**Cost Reducers:**
- Farmer loan repayment: Can recover 50-70% of input costs
- Digital delivery (PxD): Reduces logistics/training to ~$2
- Community-based extension: Lower than professional extension
- Mobile layaway (myAgro): May reduce operational costs

**Why $30 is very difficult:**
- Even with cost reducers, getting below $30 per farmer while maintaining quality is challenging
- Organizations that report lower costs often have hidden subsidies or are measuring marginal rather than full costs

- Scalability vs. efficacy tradeoffs
- Geographic generalizability

## Critical Assumptions

### For Income Doubling BOTECs:

1. **Baseline income matters:**
   - Smallholder farmer typical income: $500-1,500/year
   - Doubling $500 income: +$500 needed
   - Doubling $1,500 income: +$1,500 needed
   - Same intervention, different BOTEC outcome

2. **Timeframe matters:**
   - One-time doubling (1 year): Different from sustained
   - Multi-year cumulative: Can add up
   - Example: 40% gain for 3 years = 120% cumulative

3. **Measurement challenges:**
   - Income vs. consumption vs. revenue vs. profit
   - Farm income vs. household income
   - Gross vs. net (after input costs)
   - Cash vs. food security value

4. **Attribution:**
   - Incremental income from program vs. counterfactual
   - Selection bias (most entrepreneurial farmers enroll)
   - Spillover effects to non-participants
