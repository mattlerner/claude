# Cost-Effective Agricultural Productivity Opportunities in Sub-Saharan Africa
## Analysis for Founders Pledge

**Date:** November 6, 2025
**Analyst:** Claude
**Focus:** Opportunities that can absorb $5m+ in funding without funging other donors and meet a $30 BOTEC criterion to double income

---

## Executive Summary

This analysis evaluates agricultural productivity interventions in sub-Saharan Africa against three specific criteria:
1. **Funding absorption capacity**: $5m+ counterfactually
2. **Donor fungibility**: Must not crowd out other funders
3. **Cost-effectiveness**: $30 or less to double someone's income (BOTEC)

**Key Finding:** Based on current evidence, **no interventions clearly meet all three criteria simultaneously**. The most promising candidates fail the $30 BOTEC criterion by requiring $80-$300+ per beneficiary to achieve income gains in the 40-100% range, not full income doubling.

However, several organizations merit closer investigation for potential exceptional programs or geographies where costs may be lower.

---

## Detailed Assessment of Leading Candidates

### 1. One Acre Fund ⭐ MOST PROMISING

**Program Model:** Bundled agricultural services including financing, quality inputs (seeds, fertilizer), training, insurance, and market facilitation delivered to smallholder farmers in East and West Africa.

#### Cost-Effectiveness Analysis

**Program Costs:**
- Core bundle delivery: **$80 per farmer** (full program cost)
- After farmer repayment: **$19.59 per farmer** in required donations (2024 data)
- Basic tree program: **$20-30 per family** (one-time investment)

**Income Impacts:**
- Average farm income increase: **$124 per year** (+35% average; 2022-2024)
- 2021 results: **$104 additional profit** (+45%)
- Harvest yields: **2x increase** on supported land
- Net income increase after repayment: **40-45%** (not full doubling)

**BOTEC Verdict:** ❌ **Does not meet $30 criterion for income doubling**
- At $80 cost for 40-45% income increase, extrapolating to 100% doubling ≈ **$160-180**
- Even using the subsidized $19.59 cost, the income gain is only 35-45%, not 100%

#### Funding Capacity Assessment

**Organizational Scale:**
- Currently serves: **1.4+ million farmers** across multiple countries
- 2024 expansion: Growing to millions more
- Historical funding: Multiple rounds of $5m+ grants from various sources

**Funding Gap:**
- Global estimate: **$151 billion annual gap** for smallholder farmer support
- One Acre Fund's portion: Able to absorb **$5m+ easily**
- Required investment per farmer (OAF estimate): **$300 annually**

**Fungibility Risk:** ⚠️ **MODERATE**
- Receives funding from Gates Foundation, GiveWell (historically), Global Innovation Fund, and others
- However, still operating below capacity with massive unmet demand
- Marginal $5m likely counterfactual given scale of need

#### GiveWell Assessment
- Historically a "Standout Charity" (2020-2021, designation discontinued)
- Recent engagement: Grants for tree program RCT research (2022-2023)
- Not currently a GiveWell "top charity" for cash donations
- GiveWell estimate: Tree program has uncertain long-term income effects

#### Recommendations
1. **Request detailed cost-effectiveness data** for specific geographies or programs where costs may be lower
2. **Explore the tree program** separately - at $20-30 one-time cost, if this yields long-term income benefits, it could be promising
3. **Investigate whether any specific interventions** (e.g., specific crops, regions) show income doubling at lower costs

### 2. myAgro ⭐ PROMISING

**Program Model:** Mobile layaway savings platform enabling West African farmers to save up for seed, fertilizer, tools, and training packages via SMS-based system.

#### Cost-Effectiveness Analysis

**Reported Costs:**
- Cost per farmer: **Not explicitly stated in available sources**
- Net farming income increase: **$150-300 per farmer**
- 2020 impact analysis: **$178 more net income** (+26% income increase)

**Income Impacts:**
- Yield increase: **50-100%** (varies by program)
- Average yield increase: **78%** (2020 analysis)
- Female saving groups: **115% more food**, **$73 net profit** (>100% income increase over control)
- Income increase: **26-100%** depending on cohort

**BOTEC Verdict:** ⚠️ **POSSIBLY MEETS CRITERION - NEEDS MORE DATA**
- Female saving groups show >100% income increase
- If cost per farmer is ~$30 or less, this could meet the criterion
- **Critical data gap:** Exact cost per beneficiary not publicly available

#### Funding Capacity Assessment

**Organizational Scale:**
- Current reach: Hundreds of thousands of farmers
- 2025 goal: **1 million farmers**
- Geographic focus: Senegal, Mali, expanding to 2 new West African countries

**Recent Funding:**
- $225,000 grant (early stage)
- $2.2M unrestricted from Mulago Foundation
- Partnerships with CRS, Stromme Foundation, Aga Khan Foundation
- Partners collectively manage **4+ million rural saving group members**

**Fungibility Risk:** ✅ **LOW**
- Smaller organization than One Acre Fund
- Expansion-focused with clear room for more funding
- Partner organizations provide field infrastructure, myAgro provides tech/methodology
- $5m would likely be highly counterfactual

#### Recommendations
1. **PRIORITY: Request detailed cost-effectiveness breakdown** from myAgro
2. **Specifically ask:** Cost per farmer enrolled vs. income gains achieved
3. **Investigate:** Whether female saving group model can be scaled at comparable costs
4. **Consider:** $5m could significantly accelerate their path to 1 million farmers

### 3. Precision Development (PxD) - Digital Agricultural Advisory

**Program Model:** Mobile phone-based agricultural information and advisory services providing farmers with high-impact digital information.

#### Cost-Effectiveness Analysis

**Program Costs:**
- Cost to reach one household per year: **$1.80** (GiveWell 2020 estimate)

**Income Impacts:**
- India flagship service: **$12-19 in agricultural profits per $1 invested** (long-run estimate)
- Multiple RCT evidence showing improved production, profits, climate resilience

**BOTEC Verdict:** ✅ **COULD MEET CRITERION - IF INCOME EFFECTS SCALE**
- At $1.80/household/year, even modest income gains could be cost-effective
- If service provides $20-30+ in annual profit increases, criterion met
- However: Needs multi-year engagement; sustainability unclear

**Key Challenge:**
- Cost is per year, not one-time
- To "double income" permanently may require sustained engagement
- Over 10 years: **$18 per household** - still well under $30
- India BCR suggests this could be highly cost-effective

#### Funding Capacity Assessment

**GiveWell Engagement Timeline:**
- 2020-2021: **Standout Charity**
- March 2022: **$539,864 scoping grant**
- March 2023: **$700,000 operations grant**, but GiveWell **declined to fund implementation**
- 2023 onward: GiveWell appears to have stepped back from major funding

**Current Funding Status:**
- June 2025: **$3 million grant from Gates Foundation** for Ethiopia MoU (Dec 2025-Feb 2027)
- Signed MoU with Ethiopian Ministry of Agriculture for digital transformation

**Fungibility Risk:** ⚠️ **MODERATE-HIGH**
- Gates Foundation is major funder
- Recent MoU suggests government partnership may bring additional funding
- However, GiveWell's withdrawal suggests possible room for other funders
- **Critical question:** Why did GiveWell decline implementation funding?

#### Recommendations
1. **Investigate why GiveWell declined 2023 implementation funding** - this is a major red flag
2. **Request updated cost-effectiveness estimates** for African programs specifically
3. **Assess:** Is Gates/Ethiopia partnership absorbing all available room for funding?
4. **Consider:** May be better fit for smaller ($1-2m) exploratory grant rather than $5m

---

### 4. Government Fertilizer Subsidy Programs ❌ NOT RECOMMENDED

**Program Model:** Government-run programs providing subsidized fertilizer to smallholder farmers.

#### Cost-Effectiveness Analysis

**Program Costs:**
- Annual spending: **$1 billion across 10 African governments**
- Cost per program: **$100-160 million/year**
- Millions of beneficiaries

**Income Impacts:**
- **Malawi:** 10kg subsidized fertilizer → **$1.40-1.86/year income increase**
- **Senegal:** **11% increase in total gross margin**
- **Ghana:** Fertilizer adoption → **$332 increase in gross maize income**

**BOTEC Verdict:** ❌ **FAILS DRAMATICALLY**
- Cost-benefit ratios are poor
- Research consensus: "Costs generally outweigh benefits"
- Not suitable for effective altruism-oriented funding

#### Recommendations
**Avoid these programs entirely.** Evidence suggests poor cost-effectiveness and significant government funding already deployed.

## Alternative Approaches to Consider

### 5. CGIAR Agricultural R&D

**Evidence:**
- **Benefit-cost ratio: 10:1** overall
- Sub-Saharan Africa specific: **$6 benefit per $1 spent** (58% annual IRR)
- 2016-2020: 221 million hectares adoption, **$47 billion annual welfare gains**

**Challenges for Founders Pledge Criteria:**
- Research funding is upstream - doesn't directly "double someone's income"
- Benefits diffuse across millions over decades
- Hard to attribute marginal $5m to specific income-doubling outcomes
- Significant existing funding from governments, Gates, World Bank
- **High fungibility risk**

**Cost per QALY Estimate:** $304 (EA Forum analysis)
- Compares unfavorably to GiveWell health interventions ($45-115/DALY)

**Assessment:** ❌ **Does not meet criteria** - Wrong theory of change for "double income for $30" BOTEC

---

### 6. Agricultural Extension Services via Community Advisors

**Evidence from RCTs:**

**Cost-Effective Model (Mozambique/Tanzania):**
- Community-based, gendered extension services
- Uses trained community members
- CEA showed gendered model consistently more cost-effective than basic model
- **Challenge:** Specific cost-per-farmer data not available in search results

**Ethiopia RCT:**
- Extension services increased adoption of improved wheat varieties
- No cost-effectiveness data in search results

**Malawi RCT:**
- Demonstration plots → **14% more ISFM technology adoption**
- No cost-per-farmer data available

**Kenya RCT:**
- Agricultural + nutrition training for biofortified beans
- Newer approaches through farmer groups increase cost-effectiveness
- No specific cost data available

**Assessment:** ⚠️ **POTENTIALLY PROMISING - INSUFFICIENT DATA**
- Model could be cost-effective, but need specific implementers with clear cost data
- May be embedded in One Acre Fund or myAgro models

## Cross-Cutting Findings

### The $30 BOTEC Challenge

**Why is the criterion so difficult to meet?**

1. **Income doubling is a high bar:**
   - Most interventions show 30-80% income gains
   - 100% income increase requires either:
     - Very high-ROI interventions (rare)
     - Multi-year engagement (cumulative costs add up)

2. **Cost structure realities:**
   - Quality seeds: $10-20
   - Fertilizer: $20-40
   - Training & logistics: $20-40
   - **Total: $50-100 per farmer** for bundles

3. **Farmer loan repayment helps but doesn't solve:**
   - One Acre Fund: $43 repayment reduces $80 cost to $37 donation needed
   - Still above $30 for income doubling

4. **Comparison to GiveWell interventions:**
   - GiveDirectly: ~$1,000 per household for direct cash transfer
   - Bednets: ~$500 spent produces utility equivalent of doubling income for one year
   - **Agriculture is actually competitive** - but doesn't hit the $30 mark

### Fungibility Assessment

**Low Fungibility Opportunities:**
- myAgro (smaller scale, rapid expansion, partnership model)
- Specific geographies for One Acre Fund expansion
- New country launches for proven models

**High Fungibility Concerns:**
- CGIAR (massive government/foundation funding)
- Government subsidy programs
- Precision Development (Gates-funded Ethiopia program)

### Funding Absorption Capacity

**Organizations that can absorb $5m+:**
- ✅ One Acre Fund (easily, $150M+ budget scale)
- ✅ myAgro (transformative for 1M farmer goal)
- ⚠️ Precision Development (possible, but check Ethiopia MoU implications)
- ✅ Combinations of multiple smaller implementers

## Recommendations

### Option A: Investigate Further Before Deploying (RECOMMENDED)

**Recommended Actions:**

1. **Deep dive on myAgro cost-effectiveness:**
   - Request exact cost per farmer enrolled
   - Understand why female saving groups show >100% income gains
   - Assess whether that model can scale
   - If cost per farmer is <$30 and income doubling is proven, this could meet all criteria

2. **One Acre Fund geographic/program analysis:**
   - Some geographies or specific interventions may be more cost-effective
   - Tree program at $20-30 needs long-term income impact validation
   - Request data on highest-performing regions or crops

3. **Commission primary research:**
   - The $30 BOTEC may be achievable in specific contexts not captured by aggregate data
   - Consider funding a cost-effectiveness study of most-promising interventions
   - Budget: $200k-500k for comprehensive study could de-risk $5m deployment

### Option B: Relax the BOTEC Criterion

**Rationale:**
- **$30 for income doubling** may be unrealistic given structural costs
- **$80-100 for 50-80% income increase** may be optimal achievable outcome
- Still highly cost-effective vs. other development interventions

**If criterion relaxed to $100 for income doubling:**
- ✅ One Acre Fund becomes viable
- ✅ myAgro becomes viable
- ✅ Multiple additional implementers become options

**Cost-effectiveness comparison:**
- GiveWell bednets: ~$500 to produce utility of doubling income for one year
- Agriculture at $100 for multi-year 50-100% income boost: competitive
- Agriculture also builds productive capacity (vs. one-time transfers)

### Option C: Portfolio Approach

Rather than seeking one organization meeting all criteria, consider:

**$5M Allocation Strategy:**
- **$2M to myAgro** (conditional on cost-effectiveness validation)
- **$1.5M to One Acre Fund** (specific high-performing program/geography)
- **$1M to Precision Development** (if Ethiopia MoU has room for marginal funding)
- **$500k to research/evaluation** to identify next opportunities

**Advantages:**
- Diversifies risk
- Tests multiple theories of change
- Builds knowledge for future deployment
- Each grant large enough to be meaningful to recipient

### Option D: Wait for Better Opportunities

**Considerations:**
- Current landscape doesn't have clear $30 BOTEC opportunities
- Gates Foundation, USAID, World Bank deploying billions in this space
- Market may be relatively well-funded at top end
- **Patient capital:** Wait for new evidence, implementers, or geographies

**Risks:**
- Opportunity cost of deployed capital
- Agricultural productivity is time-sensitive (climate change, food security)
- Waiting may not produce better options

## Key Strategic Questions for Founders Pledge

### Question 1: What is the true BOTEC criterion?

Is **$30 to double income** a firm requirement, or a heuristic?

**If firm:** Current evidence suggests no programs clearly meet this threshold. Recommend waiting or commissioning research.

**If heuristic:** Several programs in the $80-150 range for 50-100% income gains could be excellent opportunities.

### Question 2: How do you value agricultural productivity vs. other cause areas?

**Agriculture advantages:**
- Builds productive capacity
- Climate resilience
- Dignity/agency vs. transfers
- Potential for sustained multi-year income gains

**Agriculture disadvantages:**
- Less cost-effective than top health interventions on DALY basis
- More complex causal chains
- Longer time horizons
- Market risks

### Question 3: What is the fungibility tolerance?

Organizations like One Acre Fund receive Gates/GiveWell funding but still have massive unmet demand.

**Is marginal $5m counterfactual?** Likely yes, given:
- $151 billion funding gap (One Acre Fund estimate)
- Organizations operating below capacity
- Geographic expansion opportunities

**Or is fungibility with any major funder disqualifying?** If so, options narrow significantly to smaller implementers like myAgro.

### Question 4: What evidence standards are required?

**Gold standard (multiple RCTs, GiveWell-level analysis):**
- Limits options to organizations with extensive research partnerships
- Precision Development, One Acre Fund (partial)
- May exclude promising implementers without research budgets

**Promising evidence (some RCTs, strong organizational track record):**
- Opens up myAgro, community extension models, others
- Accepts more uncertainty
- Could deploy with evaluation conditions

## Conclusion

**No agricultural productivity interventions in sub-Saharan Africa currently meet all three criteria simultaneously:**
1. ✅ $5m+ funding absorption: Multiple organizations qualify
2. ✅ No fungibility: Some opportunities exist (myAgro, specific expansions)
3. ❌ $30 to double income: No clear evidence of programs meeting this threshold

**However, relaxing the BOTEC to $80-150 or accepting 50-100% income gains (rather than strict doubling) opens up several viable opportunities:**
- myAgro (pending cost validation)
- One Acre Fund (specific programs/geographies)
- Precision Development (pending GiveWell decline investigation)

**Recommended path forward:**

1. **Immediate (1-2 months):** Commission detailed cost-effectiveness analysis of myAgro and One Acre Fund specific programs
2. **Short-term (3-6 months):** Based on findings, deploy $2-3M to highest-performing opportunity with evaluation conditions
3. **Medium-term (6-12 months):** Use evaluation results to inform deployment of remaining $2-3M

**Alternative:** If strict $30 BOTEC is non-negotiable, recommend allocating funding to other cause areas where this threshold is more achievable, or waiting for new evidence/implementers in agriculture space.
