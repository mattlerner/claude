# Executive Summary: Africa Agriculture Cost-Effectiveness Analysis
## For Founders Pledge - Quick Reference

**Date:** November 6, 2025

---

## The Bottom Line

**No opportunities currently meet all three criteria:**
- ✅ Can absorb $5m+ funding
- ✅ Low fungibility with other donors
- ❌ **$30 to double income** ← This is the binding constraint

**The $30 BOTEC appears unachievable in agricultural productivity** given structural costs of inputs, training, and logistics.

---

## What Does $30 Actually Buy?

| Intervention | Cost per Farmer | Income Impact | Cost to Double Income |
|-------------|----------------|---------------|---------------------|
| **One Acre Fund** | $80 ($19.59 after repayment) | +40-45% income (+$124/year) | ~$160-180 extrapolated |
| **myAgro** | Unknown (critical gap) | +26-100% income (+$150-300) | **Possibly ~$30 if high end** |
| **Precision Dev** | $1.80/year | $12-19 return per $1 | **~$18 over 10 years** |
| **Fertilizer Subsidies** | $100-160M ÷ millions | +11-30% income | $400+ (terrible) |

---

## Top Three Opportunities

### 🥇 myAgro - Highest Potential (NEEDS VALIDATION)

**Why promising:**
- Female saving groups show >100% income increase
- Reported $150-300 income gains per farmer
- Small enough that $5m is highly counterfactual
- Expanding to 1M farmers by 2025

**Critical data gap:**
- **Exact cost per farmer unknown**
- If <$30 per farmer, could meet all criteria
- If $80-150, fails BOTEC but still cost-effective

**Recommended action:**
- Request detailed cost-effectiveness breakdown
- Focus on female saving group model specifically
- Deploy $2-3M if costs validate

**Fungibility risk:** LOW (smaller org, Gates not primary funder)

---

### 🥈 One Acre Fund - Most Evidence, Above BOTEC

**Why considered:**
- Proven model serving 1.4M+ farmers
- Can absorb $5m+ easily
- Strong evidence from multiple RCTs
- Former GiveWell Standout Charity

**Why problematic:**
- $80 cost for 40-45% income gain = **fails BOTEC by ~5x**
- Even subsidized $19.59 cost doesn't yield full doubling
- Gates Foundation + other major funders = fungibility concerns

**Possible exceptions:**
- Tree program at $20-30 (but long-term income effects uncertain)
- Specific high-performing geographies
- Specific crop/intervention combinations

**Recommended action:**
- Request cost-effectiveness by program and geography
- Identify if any programs approach $30 BOTEC
- Otherwise, only viable if BOTEC relaxed

**Fungibility risk:** MODERATE-HIGH

---

### 🥉 Precision Development - Low Cost, Sustainability Questions

**Why interesting:**
- $1.80/household/year = lowest cost
- India data shows $12-19 return per $1
- Digital scalability = huge reach potential

**Why concerning:**
- **GiveWell declined implementation funding in 2023** 🚩
- Gates Foundation now primary funder ($3M Ethiopia)
- Costs are annual, not one-time
- African cost-effectiveness unclear (India data)

**Critical questions:**
- Why did GiveWell walk away?
- Is Gates/Ethiopia MoU absorbing all room for funding?
- Do benefits persist or require ongoing engagement?

**Recommended action:**
- **Investigate GiveWell decline reason first**
- Request Africa-specific cost-effectiveness data
- Consider only if GiveWell concerns addressed

**Fungibility risk:** HIGH (Gates-funded)

---

## Four Strategic Options

### Option A: Investigate & Deploy Conditionally [RECOMMENDED]

**Timeline:** 2-6 months
**Budget:** $5M + $200-500k research

**Actions:**
1. Commission detailed myAgro cost-effectiveness analysis (8 weeks)
2. Request One Acre Fund program-specific data (4 weeks)
3. If myAgro validates at <$50/farmer for income doubling:
   - Deploy $2-3M to myAgro
   - Deploy $1-2M to One Acre Fund specific program
   - Reserve $1M for evaluation/next opportunities

**Pros:** Evidence-based, preserves option value
**Cons:** 6+ month delay, research cost, may not find $30 BOTEC opportunity

---

### Option B: Relax BOTEC to $80-150

**Rationale:**
- $30 BOTEC may be structurally impossible
- $80-150 for 50-100% income gains still highly cost-effective
- Comparable to GiveDirectly, better than most development interventions

**If BOTEC relaxed:**
- ✅ One Acre Fund viable
- ✅ myAgro viable (likely)
- ✅ Multiple other implementers become options
- Can deploy $5M immediately

**Comparison:**
- GiveWell bednets: ~$500 for utility equivalent of 1-year income doubling
- Agriculture at $100-150 for multi-year 50-100% boost: competitive

**Pros:** Immediate deployment, multiple proven options
**Cons:** Doesn't meet stated criteria

---

### Option C: Portfolio Approach

**$5M Allocation:**
- $2.5M → myAgro (conditional on cost validation)
- $1.5M → One Acre Fund (best program/geography)
- $500k → Precision Development (if GiveWell concerns resolved)
- $500k → Research & evaluation

**Pros:** Diversifies risk, tests multiple models, builds knowledge
**Cons:** More management overhead, each grant smaller

---

### Option D: Allocate Elsewhere

**Rationale:**
- No programs meet $30 BOTEC
- Agriculture may not be most cost-effective cause area
- Health interventions ($45-115/DALY) are more cost-effective
- Market already has billions from Gates, USAID, World Bank

**Pros:** Deploy to higher cost-effectiveness opportunities
**Cons:** Misses potentially important agricultural productivity gains

---

## Key Unknowns That Matter

### Critical Data Gaps:

1. **myAgro exact cost per farmer**
   - If <$30: potentially meets all criteria
   - If $50-80: good but fails BOTEC
   - If $150+: not competitive

2. **Why did GiveWell decline Precision Development funding in 2023?**
   - Cost-effectiveness concerns?
   - Implementation issues?
   - Fungibility with Gates?
   - Program effectiveness in Africa?

3. **One Acre Fund marginal opportunities**
   - Which programs/geographies are most cost-effective?
   - Do any approach $30 BOTEC?

4. **General research question**
   - Are there sub-$50 interventions that achieve income doubling?
   - Do they exist but lack evidence/visibility?
   - Or is this truly not possible in agriculture?

## Comparison to Other Cause Areas

| Intervention Type | Cost-Effectiveness | Notes |
|------------------|-------------------|-------|
| **GiveWell Health Top Charities** | $45-115/DALY | More cost-effective than agriculture |
| **GiveDirectly (cash transfers)** | ~$1,000/household | Baseline comparison |
| **GiveWell Bednets (utility equiv)** | ~$500 for 1-yr income doubling | Agriculture competitive if multi-year |
| **CGIAR Ag R&D** | $304/QALY | Much worse than health |
| **One Acre Fund (if relaxed BOTEC)** | ~$80 for 40%+ multi-year | Competitive with cash |
| **myAgro (if validated)** | Possibly <$50 for 50-100%+ | Could be very competitive |

**Conclusion:** Agriculture is less cost-effective than top health interventions but competitive with cash transfers, especially if income gains are sustained over multiple years.

→ **Recommend Option D** (allocate to GiveWell top charities instead)
